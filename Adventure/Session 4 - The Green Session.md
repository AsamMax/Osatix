## Pre-Session Context

The party has just landed on [[Galapas]] with the [[Osatix]] even more damaged than before. They do not know that the tortles have a territory of their own, only that they met one strange little tortle already.

## Key Details

- **The tortles are not a major power.** They are secluded, meek, and visibly used to being overlooked.
- **The territory is hidden.** Make it feel like the party is being let into something they were never meant to find so soon.
- **The fight comes quickly.** The session should hook with combat before it becomes slow travel or politics.
- **No later setup.** Whatever happens here should resolve within the session and send the party back out with a favor, a token, or a simple path forward.
- **Give them a practical reason to go.** The party needs repair help, safe shelter, or a direct path onward, and the tortles have exactly one of those things.
- **Keep the tortles wary, not hostile.** They should feel cautious and secretive, but not like a faction that wants a fight.
- **Let the environment do some work.** Use reeds, shallow water, shells, and narrow paths to make the region feel distinct without slowing the scene down.
- **End with a clear handoff.** The party should leave with one concrete next step, not a pile of new obligations.

## Session Beats

### 1. Damaged Landing

The Osatix reaches a sheltered stretch of shoreline or lagoon near Galapas, but the damage from the voyage is obvious. The party is briefly stuck, trying to decide whether to repair the ship, scout the island, or just avoid drawing attention. They spot a group of capybaras nearby, acting nervous and repeatedly looking back toward the reeds.

One of the capybaras is wrapped in flowers and leaves. Before the party can decide what that means, a threat breaks cover and drives the animals into a panic.

### 2. Predator Attack

A predator makes it obvious the capybaras need help right now. The fight should begin before the party can settle into questioning. A single Tortle is helping a smaller group of capybaras, and the predator is a large, aggressive creature native to the island. The tortle is trying to protect the capybaras, but it's clear they are in danger. The party can choose to intervene and help the tortle fend off the predator.

### 3. Quiet Invitation

Once the predator is driven off, the tortle does not become chatty or grateful in a loud way. Instead, they stay guarded, check that the capybaras are unharmed, and gesture for the party to follow if they want answers or repairs. The path turns into narrow shallows and reed tunnels that hide the tortle territory from casual view.
